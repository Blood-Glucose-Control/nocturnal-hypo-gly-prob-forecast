**Contribution type.** This is an **E&D** submission whose contribution falls under *Benchmark Design and Benchmark Analysis* + *Evaluation Methodology and Metrics*, not a new dataset or a new SOTA model. Rev1(S5) states they hope this paper becomes the *standard* for glucose-level forecasting in the future. This implies that the vast majority of hundreds of glucose forecasting publications do not meet this standard and a publication in a top venue would have broad reaching impact in other adjacent fields methodological practices. We also understand that a publication with broad impact outside of NeurIPS but without sufficent novelty to the ML community is not enough for acceptance at this top venue. **However**, given that the contribution is focused on Bench Design + Eval Metrics our paper **is novel** in that it:
    1. Provides a problem formulation that is challenging and **unique in combination of its metrics**, the authors of this paper are unaware of any problem where there is a critical uncontrived need for optimizing both distributional and shape metrics, and where the median forecast of a distribution is just as important as forecasting the tails.
    2. Identifies a critical issue with most publically released TSFMs not natively supporting a broad set of quantiles that are critical in this setting for AUROC/AUPRC.
    3. Identifies a critical issue of many TSFMs not natively supporting co-variates.
    4. Draws attention to TSFM researchers about the underutilized, widely available and rapidly growing resource of continuous glucose moniotring data.
    5. Demonstrates a forecasting problem that advantages architectures capable of handling covariates and long context windows.

Rev2(S2) main concern about the **clarity** of what is "new" is completely fair. However this is a matter of **clarity** not a matter of actually lacking novelty for the E&D track. With minor revisions for a camera-ready version of our paper will state this more clearly.

We submitted this paper to the **E&D** track precisely because there are no existing solutions that adequately solve this real problem. We strongly believe that this completely novel framing of diabetes forecasting will provide a long lasting and rich proving ground for TSFM researchers. We realize there are still refinements to be made as a benchmark, this will be an iterative process as we get more valuable feedback from the machine learning community. However, if we were to pose the question **Which TSFM is currently best at both shape and probabilistic forecasting?** we don't think anyone could provide a satisfying answer, especially in the context of rare event detection in the tails of the forecasted prob. distribution.

Our central finding is a **cross-metric double dissociation**: the model that is significantly best on point/prob. scores (chronos2 on RMSE/WQL) is significantly *worse* on trajectory shape (DILATE) and on hypoglycemia detection (AUROC/AUPRC), where continuous-output models (Moirai/Toto/PatchTST) lead. The Spearman correlation between the RMSE ranking and the shape/detection rankings is only **−0.05 to −0.52** (vs +0.93–0.98 for RMSE/WQL). The meta-review's own summary states this finding correctly, a core message to the diabetes forecasting community **optimizing point accuracy does not optimize clinical event detection or provide more interpretable shape forcasts.**

**On the meta-review.** One inaccuracy in the meta-review about the benchmark design (exclusive univariate CGM modeling), in fact every model is evaluated glucose-only, and any model architecture that has covariates support is also assessed in a controlled per-model ablation.

**Rebuttal-window work.** We added analyses using patient-clustered resampling. Paired significance tests, comparisons are significant under 10,000-bootstrap patient clustering; confusion matrices; midnight-anchoring sensitivity; glucose-only fairness leaderboard (architecture spread 0.50–0.64 RMSE vs cov. gain 0.07–0.10); subgroup analysis by age and sex; ZS vs FT contamination check.
