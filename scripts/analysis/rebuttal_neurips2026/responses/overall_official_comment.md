We thank the AC. We first note clarify a few factual points about the meta-review, then answer its four questions with **new analyses added during rebuttal** (would be reproducible in newly released code; all use patient-clustered resampling, see below).

**Q1 Univariate vs. multimodal realism with Correction** The meta-review states "Exclusively Univariate CGM Modeling" and "Univariate Framing vs. Multimodal Clinical Realism" asking "How do you justify framing long-horizon nocturnal forecasting strictly around univariate CGM history, given that overnight glucose trajectories are heavily influenced by evening insulin boluses, meals, and physical activity?"

**Response:** we do not justify exclusively using univariate-only CGM modeling. The benchmark is **not univariate-only**: we report glucose-only, +insulin (I), and +insulin+carbs (IC) for every covariate-capable model. As Rev3(S3) states: "**Some models are evaluated with insulin and carbohydrate covariates** while others are effectively restricted to glucose-only settings. [...] improvements attributed to architecture may partially reflect access to additional information rather than superior modeling capability. The benchmark would benefit from a [...] identical inputs **whenever possible.**" However, it is in fact the case with our benchmark, **if a TSFM or other architecture supported handling covariates, they were included** in the benchmark. We will make this more clear in the paper methodology section.
**New 1:** new statistical comparisons are discussed in the response to Rev3(S3), regarding model to model comparisons using paired patient-cluster bootstrap analysis with a **glucose-only** leaderboard. If desired by reviwers we can separate tables into covariate and non-covariate leaderboards for camera-ready version.
**New 2:** on the **glucose-only** leaderboard (identical input for all 10 models), the architecture spread is **0.50–0.64 RMSE** per dataset, whereas the *median* within-model gain from adding covariates is only **+0.07–0.10 RMSE** (paired patient-cluster bootstrap; chronos2 gains most, +0.09–0.18, all CIs exclude 0). So covariate access does **not** exclusively drive the ranking, architecture also contributes, however this also identifies an important issue in TSFM research where many architectures do no support covariates.

**Q2: Divergent metrics & alarm utility.** The meta-review's own summary states our central finding: rankings "diverge significantly on shape-sensitive (DILATE) and classification (AUROC/AUPRC) metrics." We now establish this **statistically**: chronos2 is significantly best on RMSE/WQL yet significantly *worse* than Moirai/Toto/PatchTST on DILATE-shape and on hypo detection (AUROC/AUPRC). A paired **patient-clustered** double dissociation (95% CIs exclude 0 on DCLP3/IOBP2; Spearman between the RMSE ranking and the shape/detection rankings is −0.05 to −0.52, vs +0.93–0.98 for RMSE/WQL). **Interpretation for alarm systems**: the model should be chosen by the *clinical* metric, not by RMSE. On alarm utility itself: at 90% sensitivity false alarms run **45–72 per 100 nights** (PPV 0.14–0.43, base-rate dependent); at the Youden point PPV reaches **0.55–0.63** at 44–68% recall. Long-horizon alarms have significant room for improvement at low false-alarm rates which is a *finding*, not a limitation we hide, because it motivates publishing this benchmark to inspire future research. Still, if evaluated in clincial settings as is, we expect it is likely to reduce harm (a small uneeded intervention is less severe than a 3AM wake-up).

**Q3: Fair adaptation.** Context window (512), forecast horizon (96), preprocessing, holdout splits, and the midnight-anchored episode set are **identical across all models**; only architecture-native fine-tuning differs. Our repo already contains a context-length ablation (same anchors across ctx). New: **zero-shot vs. fine-tuned** paired tests show fine-tuning significantly helps nearly every foundation model (patient-cluster CIs exclude 0), confirming adaptation was actually applied.

**Q4: Pretraining contamination.** Before including each TSFM we manually reviewed its publication and documented pretraining corpus (Chronos, Moirai, TimesFM, Moment, Toto): **to our knowledge, none report any diabetes or CGM data**. We surface this as a *finding*, not just a negative check, the major TSFM pretraining corpora entirely omit CGM, a large-scale (47.5M readings in this benchmark alone), these datasets have high real world impact with rich open problems; the TSFM community is underutilizing a valuable data source. The four JAEB datasets are **credentialed/gated**. Empirically, **zero-shot is significantly worse than fine-tuned** on 23/24 model/dataset cells,the opposite of what memorization would produce.
